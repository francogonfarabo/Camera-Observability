How this board decides things

Every number here comes from one row per camera — the assessor's latest verdict on it.

Camera states

Three of these are the assessor's verdict and are stored with the reading. The fourth, out of date, is decided in the browser from how old that reading is, and is never written anywhere — which is why it sits over a status rather than replacing one.

Offline
Any one of: • the HLS playlist does not exist;
• FFMPEG restarts exceed the critical threshold (default 60 in 5 minutes);
• the HLS playlist is stale or missing and the latest JPG is stale or missing (stale meaning older than 100 seconds). Note the and in the third clause — it is what separates offline from degraded. One dead artifact is an impairment; both are an outage.
Degraded
None of the offline conditions, but any one of: • FFMPEG restarts exceed the warning threshold (default 30 in 5 minutes);
• the HLS playlist is older than 100 seconds;
• the latest JPG is older than 100 seconds. Still reporting, just not well. The two restart thresholds are per-deployment settings; the 100-second window is fixed in the assessor.
Online
None of the above is true.
Out of date
assessed_at is more than 1 hour old. The assessor runs every 30 minutes, so this means two consecutive runs produced nothing for this camera. Not a status. A block keeps the colour and the shape of whatever it was last assessed as, loses its saturation, and gains a dashed ring: this was offline, and we have not heard since is two facts, and a fourth flat colour would have destroyed the first to show the second. Front-end only — nothing persists it, and it disappears the moment a fresh row arrives. It marks cameras and nothing else: a center square answers how much of the site is working, which a reading's age does not change the answer to.

Order matters: offline is tested first, so a camera meeting both sets of conditions is offline, not degraded. Nothing in this section confirms a picture — only that the pipeline behind it is producing files on time.

Center colors

Only the two ends are precise. Green means nothing is wrong; red means everything is. Everything in between is one orange bucket on purpose — the square answers does this center need looking at, and the row underneath answers how much.

All cameras offline
Every camera in the center has stopped reporting. offline = total. The site is blind. That is almost always one cause — power, network, the recorder — rather than a list of separate faults, which is why it gets its own colour instead of being the top of a scale.
Some offline or degraded
Anything short of both extremes: one impaired camera among healthy ones, half the site down, nine of ten offline with the tenth impaired, every camera degraded but still reporting. offline < total and offline + degraded > 0. Deliberately ungraded. A colour that tried to separate "one camera is a bit soft" from "most of the site is dark" would be asking people to read a hue to three decimal places; the counts on the center's row do that job in numbers, and the hover card says it in words.
All cameras healthy
Nothing offline and nothing impaired. offline = 0 and degraded = 0. Green is a clean bill of health, not merely "nothing is dark". It matches the All cameras healthy status filter and the first KPI exactly, so the squares, the filter and the headline count never disagree about how many centers are fine.

Color is never the only cue: offline blocks carry an ✕ and degraded blocks a diagonal hatch, so the three states survive a colorblind reader, a projector and a grayscale print.

Staleness never reaches this rule. A center's colour is about how much of the site is working, and how old a reading is asks a different question of a different object — so the squares above stay a three-state overview, and the out-of-date treatment lives on the camera blocks inside each center, on the count beside them, and in the Status filter.

Sorting

Defect Rate
(offline + degraded) ÷ total cameras, per center, as a percentage. Highest first by default. Rate, not count: a center with 3 of 8 cameras down is in worse shape than one with 4 of 40, and only the rate says so.
Defect Count
offline + degraded — the absolute number of cameras not fully healthy. Highest first by default. Count, not rate: this is the one that maps to how many visits or work orders the day actually holds.
Outage Time
The oldest current outage in the center: max(now − last healthy heartbeat) across its offline cameras. Longest first by default. Degraded cameras are excluded — they are still reporting, so they have no outage clock running. Centers with nothing offline sort last.
Center Name
Alphabetical, A–Z by default.
Store Number
Numeric on the store number, lowest first by default.

Ties fall back to center name A–Z, in both directions. The card shows whichever number you sorted by, so the order is always checkable against something on screen.

Filtering

Status
What the center's cameras are doing right now: any issue, offline specifically, degraded specifically, stale specifically, or everything healthy. Has any issue and All cameras healthy are the two halves of the centers strip — the first is every non-green square, the second is every green one. Has stale cameras sits alongside those rather than between them: a reading can be out of date whatever it says, so it overlaps both. Values inside a category are ORed, so picking it together with Has offline cameras asks for centers matching either, not both.
Brand
The operating brand the center trades under.
Payment model
Who pays for the camera service — parent-pay (families subscribe) or center-pay (the center covers it).
Parent access
Whether parents can watch this center's cameras (parent-facing) or the feeds are staff-only (internal). Independent of who pays.
Region
The district grouping a regional manager owns. Regions group states, so a region can span more than one.
Country
United States or Canada. It's the outermost scope: changing it drops any state, city, region or brand it leaves with nothing to match.
State / Province
Where the center sits. The list is grouped under United States and Canada rather than gated behind Country: a state name is unambiguous on its own, so a gate would cost every reader two clicks to fix what a heading fixes for free. City is gated, because a city name is not unambiguous — Springfield and Rochester are each two different centers in this assignment.
City
Available once a state is chosen — an ungated city list is every city you can see, which is a wall rather than a filter.
Timezone
The center's local timezone, written out rather than abbreviated — the whole point of this filter is scheduling work outside opening hours, so the zone has to be readable without decoding it.

Filters don't have to be applied in order. Each list is built from the centers that pass your other choices, and the number beside an option is how many centers you'd be left with — so choosing a state narrows the city list on its own, and an option that would empty the board is greyed out instead of offered. Within one category you can pick as many values as you like and they're ORed together (Texas or Oklahoma); separate categories are ANDed (Texas, and parent-pay). The counts also respect whatever is in the search box.
