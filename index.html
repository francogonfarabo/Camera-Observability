How this board decides things

Every number here comes from one row per camera — the assessor's latest verdict on it.

Camera states

Three of these are the assessor's verdict and are stored with the reading. The fourth, out of date, is decided in the browser from how old that reading is, and is never written anywhere — which is why it sits over a status rather than replacing one.

Offline
Any one of: • the HLS playlist does not exist;
• FFMPEG restarts exceed the critical threshold (default 60 in 5 minutes);
• the HLS playlist is stale or missing and the latest JPG is stale or missing (stale meaning older than 100 seconds). Note the and in the third clause — it is what separates offline from degraded. One dead artifact is an impairment; both are an outage.
Degraded
None of the offline conditions, but any one of: • FFMPEG restarts exceed the warning threshold (default 30 in 5 minutes);
• the HLS playlist is older than 100 seconds;
• the latest JPG is older than 100 seconds. Still reporting, just not well. The two restart thresholds are per-deployment settings; the 100-second window is fixed in the assessor.
Online
None of the above is true.
Out of date
assessed_at is more than 1 hour old. The assessor runs every 30 minutes, so this means two consecutive runs produced nothing for this camera. Not a status. A block keeps the colour and the shape of whatever it was last assessed as, loses its saturation, and gains a dashed ring: this was offline, and we have not heard since is two facts, and a fourth flat colour would have destroyed the first to show the second. Front-end only — nothing persists it, and it disappears the moment a fresh row arrives. It marks cameras and nothing else: a center square answers how much of the site is working, which a reading's age does not change the answer to.

Order matters: offline is tested first, so a camera meeting both sets of conditions is offline, not degraded. Nothing in this section confirms a picture — only that the pipeline behind it is producing files on time.

The centers band

The three center states are the same three the rest of this board uses, and they never change: red only when every camera in a center is offline, green only when nothing is offline and nothing impaired, orange for everything in between. What changes with the size of your assignment is not the colours — it is what one cell is.

Fully down
offline = total. The site is blind — no parent at that center can see anything. Almost always one cause (power, network, the recorder) rather than a list of separate faults, which is why it gets its own colour instead of being the top of a scale. It leads the sort at every zoom, so the top-left cell is always the worst thing you have.
Needs attention
offline < total and offline + degraded > 0. One impaired camera among healthy ones, half the site down, nine of ten offline with the tenth impaired. Deliberately ungraded. The square answers does this center need looking at; the row underneath answers how much, in numbers.
All reporting
offline = 0 and degraded = 0. Exactly the same rule as the All cameras healthy status filter and the first KPI, so the squares, the filter and the headline count never disagree.
One cell is a zoom level, not a center
The Center / State / Brand control above the cells switches between them, and AUTO picks the finest one that still draws at a readable size inside three rows: a square per center for a few hundred, a tile per state across the whole 1,231. Twelve hundred squares at a size anyone can read is eleven rows of nothing. Squares stop shrinking at 13px, because below that the hatch is mud and the ✕ a smudge, and colour on its own is not an encoding.
A group is a mix, so it gets a tile
At any zoom above center, a cell holds many centers and is therefore not in one of the three states — it is some of each. The tile names the group, says how many of its centers need attention, and splits the bar inside it into the same three colours. A tile rather than a square for two reasons. At this zoom the name is most of the value — you are looking for Ohio, not decoding a swatch — and a mix cannot honestly be painted one colour. Note that a single dark site inside seventy would round to nothing in that bar, so a non-zero slice never shrinks below a visible sliver. Click a tile to filter the board to that group; ⌘ or Ctrl-click adds a second. The breadcrumb above shows where you are and takes you back.
Folded away
Centers with nothing wrong collapse into one chip — 541 centers all reporting — which expands on click. Cells are sorted worst-first, so if more remain than three rows will hold, what is hidden is always the least urgent and a chip says how many.
What a filter does to the band
While one cell can still be one center, the band keeps drawing the whole assignment and empties the excluded squares to a small neutral dot, so nothing moves. Emptied rather than faded: lightness is doing no work here, and a dimmed red ✕ beside a full-strength orange hatch is a coin toss in grayscale. Once there are more centers than three rows can hold, holding position is no longer on offer — so filtering re-scopes the band instead, it re-tiers inside what is left, and a chip reports how many dropped out. A group tile that only partly matches shows the matching share as a blue line above its bar.

Color is never the only cue: an offline square carries an ✕ and a partly-affected one a diagonal hatch, so the three states survive a colorblind reader, a projector and a grayscale print.

Staleness never reaches this rule. A cell's colour is about how much of the fleet under it is working, and how old a reading is asks a different question of a different object — so the out-of-date treatment lives on the camera blocks inside each center, on the count beside them, and in its own filter.

Sorting

Defect Rate
(offline + degraded) ÷ total cameras, per center, as a percentage. Highest first by default. Rate, not count: a center with 3 of 8 cameras down is in worse shape than one with 4 of 40, and only the rate says so.
Defect Count
offline + degraded — the absolute number of cameras not fully healthy. Highest first by default. Count, not rate: this is the one that maps to how many visits or work orders the day actually holds.
Outage Time
The oldest current outage in the center: max(now − last healthy heartbeat) across its offline cameras. Longest first by default. Degraded cameras are excluded — they are still reporting, so they have no outage clock running. Centers with nothing offline sort last.
Center Name
Alphabetical, A–Z by default.
Store Number
Numeric on the store number, lowest first by default.

Ties fall back to center name A–Z, in both directions. The card shows whichever number you sorted by, so the order is always checkable against something on screen.

Filtering

Out-of-date readings
A switch, not a list — the only category on this panel that is a yes/no. On, it keeps the centers holding at least one camera whose assessment has gone stale. It sits above the others because it outranks them: a reading being out of date is a statement about the reading, not a fourth thing a camera can be, and everything the rest of the panel filters on is a claim about the past for those centers. It used to be one option inside Status, where it read as a status.
Center status
The square's own verdict, as a filter: Fully down, Needs attention, All reporting. The three are mutually exclusive and their counts sum to the whole assignment. It is read straight off the same rule that colours the square, so the filter and the mark cannot disagree. There is no Has any issue option: that is Fully down and Needs attention chosen together, which is what the first KPI tile selects.
Camera health
A different question from the one above — Has offline cameras, Has degraded cameras — and deliberately not exclusive with it, since a center needing attention can hold both. Values inside a category are ORed, so choosing both asks for centers matching either, not both.
Brand
The operating brand the center trades under. About one site in twelve belongs to no chain and has an empty brand field. Those are offered as No brand — last in the list, because it is the absence of a brand rather than one more of them — and they get their own group in Your centers for the same reason. "No brand" and not "Independent": the record says the field is empty, which is not the same claim.
Payment model
Who pays for the camera service — parent-pay (families subscribe) or center-pay (the center covers it).
Parent access
Whether parents can watch this center's cameras (parent-facing) or the feeds are staff-only (internal). Independent of who pays.
Country
United States or Canada. It's the outermost scope: changing it drops any state, city or brand it leaves with nothing to match.
State / Province
Where the center sits. The list is grouped under United States and Canada rather than gated behind Country: a state name is unambiguous on its own, so a gate would cost every reader two clicks to fix what a heading fixes for free. City is gated, because a city name is not unambiguous — Springfield and Rochester are each two different centers in this assignment.
City
Available once a state is chosen — an ungated city list is every city you can see, which is a wall rather than a filter.

Filters don't have to be applied in order. Each list is built from the centers that pass your other choices, and the number beside an option is how many centers you'd be left with — so choosing a state narrows the city list on its own, and an option that would empty the board is greyed out instead of offered. Within one category you can pick as many values as you like and they're ORed together (Texas or Oklahoma); separate categories are ANDed (Texas, and parent-pay). The counts also respect whatever is in the search box.
