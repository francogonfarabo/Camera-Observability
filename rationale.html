Camera health: two versions, and how they got here

A live board that tells a center admin, a regional manager, or an exec which cameras are down right now — built at two altitudes, and steered at each turn by specific calls rather than left to default choices.

How this took shape

The decisions below were Franco's; this is where each one landed in the design.

1

Set the frame before any layout existed. Light theme only (the dark mockup as reference, not a target), a 1–20 center scale, a three-value legend, and "a working prototype plus these notes" as the deliverable — settled by a few answers before a single row was drawn.

2

Called for a second version and wrote its brief. That concept spec — no center selected until one is chosen, two entry points, problems collapsed by default, no streaming as a default state, a named diagnosis when failures share a cause — is where most of what's different about version B comes from.

3

Renamed the feature from "Current Status" to "Camera health" everywhere, ahead of the fork, so neither version carries the old name.

4

Reset version B's mechanics with reference images mid-build: the way in became a toggle inside the center-search field rather than a separate header button; camera detail moved from tiles into version A's status squares; a real dashboard screen was added as what you land on before choosing a center.

5

Two corrections after seeing it live. The greyed-out Cameras nav item was reversed — it should look identical to its siblings, just not navigate — and the center-search dropdown was turned into a non-functional reference: selecting a row does nothing, for now.

6

Asked for saved filters, in B only. Not a request to bring A and B back into parity — a deliberate call that the save/reapply workflow belongs to the version built for triage across many centers, not the single-center one.

7

Cut B back to one list and one destination. The problems-first / all-centers switch went — every center is listed and the sort does that job — and every route into a single center's camera page went with it. Both were in the original concept spec; both were removed after seeing them built, on the grounds that a triage board should be the answer rather than a menu.

8

Pulled the recorder thread out of B. The shared-recorder diagnosis banner is gone from every card, and the modal drops Recorder, Camera ID, and Last frame received down to Status, duration, Last checked, and Room. The recorder field stays in the data model — it still drives the hover tooltip — but nothing in the board argues from it anymore.

9

Brought B's updates back to A, with one explicit exception. The consolidated filter dropdown, filter-linked KPIs with the calm-zero treatment, saved views, the trimmed drawer, and the softened copy all landed in A too. Nested's own This center / All centers toggle — the thing that most defines it against B — was named as the one piece to keep untouched rather than let a global port quietly erase it.

10

Chose A, and hid B rather than deleting it. Now that A carries every update B pioneered, B's file still builds and still deploys — it's simply no longer linked from the switcher. Reversible if the account-altitude model turns out to matter later; invisible if it doesn't.

11

Gave camera health its own place in the rail, instead of a sub-tab of Cameras. A new top-level item, Monitoring, set first and apart from the center-scoped section icons by a divider — because "camera health across every center" is a different kind of question than anything Cameras, Dashboard, or Users answer, and burying it inside one center-scoped section was never quite honest about that. Cameras keeps its icon but stops claiming the page; it now sits inert like the other placeholder sections.

12

Asked for a comments resource — a plain textbox, per center. Not a comment thread: no author, no timestamp, nothing to reply to. A small button beside the center's meta line expands one textarea in place. Browser-local, and outside "Reset data" for the same reason saved views are — it's the user's own annotation, not sample data.

13

Asked for the note state to be visible, not just discoverable. A dot on the button turned out to be too quiet to answer "have I already looked at this one?". The chip now goes blue-tinted and bold with its page glyph filled in — colour and shape, the same rule the status blocks follow — and the same mark rides on that center's block in the strip. The note's text stays out of the row (there is no room for it at the narrowest column, and a chip that resizes with its content makes the whole row jump); it surfaces on hover, and again in the camera drawer, where a note about the center is exactly the context you want while diagnosing one of its cameras.

14

Called for a centers strip, wired to the search field. Every center the account can see, one block each, in the same encoding the cameras use one level down. The brief was explicit that it should feel connected to the toolbar rather than sit above it as one more chart — so the strip and the search/filter row are one card, and a click writes that center into the field directly beneath it. Also: Monitoring stops advertising four sections it doesn't have yet, and an offline camera's drawer stops looking like a healthy one's.

15

Made the strip the altitude, and cut everything it made redundant. The This center / All centers toggle is gone — kept as A's defining feature back in step 9, and now unnecessary, because a strip showing all fifty-two centers already says which altitude you are at. In its place a dismissible line states it in words. The platform's center picker stays in the header but disabled: choosing a center happens on the Dashboard, and a control that vanishes when you change section reads as a bug where one that greys out reads as "not applicable here". Also cut in the same pass: the comfortable/compact switch (one density, no control), the arrow that pointed from a picked block to the search field (the highlight and the filled field already say it), the link icon beside every center name, and the "all clear" banner. Filters and sort split into two dropdowns at the far right, because the active sort was invisible while it lived inside a panel called Filters. The search box now holds a draft until you press Search or Enter, instead of querying on every keystroke.

Duplicate readouts went too: "N need attention" beside the strip title (the first KPI says it), the camera-level legend above the list (the strip's legend covers the same encoding), the row of counts under the toolbar when nothing is filtered, and the drawer's status/duration/room table — the title carries the room and the headline carries the duration, so only the camera ID was left worth a row.

16

Set the prototype's assignment to 52 centers. Fourteen was a demo; fifty-two is a regional manager's actual list, and it is what justifies every piece of machinery on the page — the brand-grouped strip, pagination, saved views, the search-then-submit box. It also settles the layout question: at 52 centers the strip is three rows of brand groups, not a wall, and the rail of squares still reads at a glance.

17

Made the prototype open on the Dashboard, not on Monitoring. The reasoning was the point of the whole exercise: Monitoring is a new section, so the only way to judge it is to arrive from somewhere that works the old way. So the prototype now starts on a center's dashboard with the header picker live and one center's numbers on screen; the Cameras cell there is the door into Monitoring, which is also where someone would really notice cameras. Crossing over, the picker empties and locks — an emptied, greyed field is the clearest available statement that the page stopped being about one center — and the notice names the center still selected behind you on the Dashboard. The dashboard screen is deliberately thin and says so on its face: it stands in for the existing product.

In the same pass: sorting gained a direction, with both ends named in the sorted field's own words ("Most issues first" / "Fewest issues first", "A–Z" / "Z–A") rather than a bare asc/desc the reader has to translate; rows went back to the comfortable density (the compact one was too small to read at a glance); the coloured left edge came off the cards; the "Ranking changed — reorder" pill came off entirely, though the wait it announced stays; the board fills the window's width; and the rail and sub-tab icons grew, with their labels pinned at 9px and 11px.

18

Sent screenshots of the real product and asked for the shell to match them. So the dashboard is now that screen rather than a sketch of one: the blue welcome band with its orange new-requests badge, the date and 7-day-snapshot strip, four divided stat cells with their little "?" buttons and the active-users donut, the dark Quick Tip card, the resource-guide article, and the Cameras / Saved Clips tabs over a panel of camera tiles — which shows the product's own "Error! Unable to load requested cameras" state when no center is chosen. The rail gained the sections the product actually has (Requests, Supervision, Reactions, Success).

Two details earn their keep beyond fidelity. The camera tiles render that center's real statuses, so an offline camera shows up as a dead tile on the dashboard — which is exactly where someone first notices, one center at a time, that a camera is not coming back; the link under the panel carries that count into camera health. And the center picker now behaves like the product's: a white pill holding the center name over its brand, a dropdown with the current center pinned green at the top and "Search all centers" at the bottom, and — on a phone — a field that takes over the whole bar while you search, avatar stepping aside, results filling the screen. The phone also gets the rail back behind a hamburger, since without it there was no way between Dashboard and Monitoring at all.

Two versions

A · Nested — a sub-nav under its own rail item

Camera health as the only sub-tab of a dedicated Monitoring rail item — originally filed under Cameras, and originally shown beside four placeholder sub-tabs that have since gone: a section that holds one thing should say so rather than advertise a roadmap. Cheapest to ship of the two. It no longer carries a scope toggle either: the page is always the whole assignment, the header's center picker empties and greys out while you are in it, and a strip of all 52 centers makes the altitude self-evident. The prototype opens on a center's Dashboard so that change of state is something you watch happen rather than something you read about.

B · Toggle — a scope above center selection

Camera health as the one thing that exists at account altitude, reached by a toggle in the header's search field. No center selected, center-scoped nav hidden. Resolves the seam honestly, at the cost of a scope the app has never had before.

B is the right long-term model; A is the right first ship. If the toggle version is likely within two quarters, build B now — retrofitting a scope later means re-teaching the navigation twice.

Layout

One row per center in both versions: identity, a fixed column of aligned status counts, then a wrapping grid of camera squares. Status is colour plus texture, never colour alone — red and green is the pair that fails for red-green colour blindness, and this board's whole meaning rides on exactly that pair.

The counts get their own column because sharing it with identity makes them wrap unpredictably; given a column, ten rows becomes one scannable ladder of digits. Rows carry no coloured left edge: the counts column and the squares already state the severity twice, and a third encoding of it was just stripes.

Above the list, version A adds a centers strip: the same three-way encoding applied one level up, one block per center the account can see, grouped by brand so the hunt for a particular site is bounded. It is not a separate card — it shares one card with the search and filter row, because its real job is navigational: clicking a block writes that center's name into the field below it and the list narrows to that center. Two rules keep that promise honest. A click drops exactly the filters that would have excluded the center it just picked, so a pick never lands on an empty list. And the search text is excluded from the strip's own dimming — filters dim the blocks they rule out, but letting the search dim them would black out fifty-one blocks the moment you clicked the fifty-second, which is the opposite of what an overview is for.

The prototype's user is assigned 52 centers across eight brands. At that size the strip becomes three rows of brand-labelled groups rather than an undifferentiated wall, which is why the grouping exists at all: it bounds the hunt for one site to six or seven squares. Center rows sit at one density — the comfortable one, with 20px squares — since a density switch is a preference the page can simply make well once. The board fills the window's full width: the camera grids are what benefits from every pixel, because a wider row is fewer wrapped rows per center.

What's different between them

Both versions now agree on more than they did: every center is always listed, saved views exist in both, and neither one filters the page down to a single center. What remains different is how each one states its altitude, and where the detail opens.

  • B states altitude with a control; A states it with the page. B's search field reads "All my centers" with a filled blue chip while camera health is on. A dropped its scope toggle entirely — the strip of every center, plus one dismissible line, does that job, and the header's center picker greys out for as long as you are in Monitoring.
  • B hides the center-scoped nav; A leaves it. In B, absent nav reads as a different altitude. A keeps the rail intact and lets Dashboard lead it, since Dashboard is where a center actually gets picked.
  • Cameras in the rail looks normal but isn't a route — in both, every path to a camera runs through camera health, so there's one door, not two.
  • Detail opens in a modal in B, a drawer in A — a modal reads as the harder stop that suits triage; a drawer lets the board stay readable behind it.
  • Every center is listed, always. No problems-first / all-centers switch and no collapsed healthy row: the sort decides what floats to the top, so there is one list rather than a mode to be in. Problem centers show their whole grid, not just the broken cameras — four red among twenty green tells a different story than four red alone.
  • The row holds at 100 cameras. A 100-camera site wraps to three tidy rows on desktop and seven on a phone, with no overflow at any width and the keyboard grid still a single tab stop. Squares stay one fixed size at every count on purpose: scaling them down for large sites would make a red square mean something different from card to card, and comparing centers at a glance is the whole job.
  • Both can save a view. Name the current filters, search and sort; reapply or delete it from a chip row. Browser-local (localStorage), not an account setting. This began as B's alone and was ported to A once A became the version to ship.
  • Single-center users see none of this — nothing to aggregate, so no altitude to state.

There is no center view. B has exactly two screens: the cold-start dashboard and camera health. Center names are labels, not links; the camera modal offers my.wmg and nothing else. This is the sharper reading of "camera health is the only place to check cameras" — if drilling into a single center's camera page were one click away, the board would be a menu rather than the answer.

The refresh contract

  • The countdown is visible and the subtitle states data age — "read 12s ago".
  • Pause is real and labeled: "showing a frozen view," not a stopped clock.
  • It stops on its own when the tab is hidden, a detail view is open, or someone is typing a filter.
  • Ranking never changes under the cursor. A reorder waits, silently, until the pointer and the keyboard have both left the list. It used to announce the wait with a pill; the pill turned out to cost more attention than the wait it was explaining.
  • Failure is stated, not hidden — an amber banner names the data's age and offers retry; the board never goes blank.
  • Nothing focused or hovered gets replaced. The centers strip is built once and then patched attribute-by-attribute on every read, and a re-render that does replace a camera block re-anchors the hover card to the block that took its place instead of stranding it on the page.
  • Screen readers get one polite summary per read, not per-block noise.

Keyboard and access

  • Each center's grid is one tab stop with roving focus — arrows move between cameras, not 20 separate stops per center.
  • Every square's accessible name includes the duration: "Camera 14, Nap room B, offline for 1h 05m".
  • The hover card also appears on focus. Informational text sits at 4.5:1 contrast or better.

Notes for engineering

  • Don't query the assessment log directly per poll. Maintain a camera_current_status projection — one row per camera — so sorting and paginating is an indexed read, not a windowed scan.
  • Compute counts in SQL, per page. Never ship thousands of camera rows to count client-side.
  • Distinguish "offline" from "we haven't heard." A camera past its heartbeat window is unknown, not healthy — see question 2.
  • Scope every query server-side by the user's access; the UI toggle is a convenience, never the security boundary.

Open questions

1

Should the Camera health toggle carry a count?

The concept spec called a badge the main discoverability driver on a good day; the reference image had none, so it shipped without one. A middle option: show the number only when non-zero.

2

What happens to a camera whose last heartbeat is over 30 minutes old?

Right now it keeps its last status, which quietly counts a silent camera as healthy. Recommend a fourth "not reporting" state — but that widens the three-value legend.

3

Where does someone go once camera health tells them a center is broken?

B deliberately has no route into a single center, so the next step happens outside this screen — on my.wmg, or by leaving camera health and picking the center the normal way. Worth confirming that matches how the work actually continues, since it is the one thing the board stops short of.

4

Timestamps in the viewer's timezone, or the center's?

Relative times ("27m ago") dodge the question for now; absolute timestamps will need an answer.

5

Is camera order within a center stable and meaningful?

If not, ordering by room would beat whatever order the log happens to return.

6

Does "degraded" carry a reason?

Low bitrate versus dropped frames versus a bad angle is the difference between calling the vendor and nudging the camera.

History, saved-clip playback, and an admin display mode are out of scope for this pass. The sub-nav used to reserve Live, History, Saved Clips, and Admin Display as decorative tabs; they have been removed. Monitoring holds one section, and showing four it doesn't have reads as a roadmap rather than a product — their eventual home can be invented when they exist.
