Camera health: two versions, and how they got here

A live board that tells a center admin, a regional manager, or an exec which cameras are down right now — built at two altitudes, and steered at each turn by specific calls rather than left to default choices.

How this took shape

The decisions below were Franco's; this is where each one landed in the design.

1

Set the frame before any layout existed. Light theme only (the dark mockup as reference, not a target), a 1–20 center scale, a three-value legend, and "a working prototype plus these notes" as the deliverable — settled by a few answers before a single row was drawn.

2

Called for a second version and wrote its brief. That concept spec — no center selected until one is chosen, two entry points, problems collapsed by default, no streaming as a default state, a named diagnosis when failures share a cause — is where most of what's different about version B comes from.

3

Renamed the feature from "Current Status" to "Camera health" everywhere, ahead of the fork, so neither version carries the old name.

4

Reset version B's mechanics with reference images mid-build: the way in became a toggle inside the center-search field rather than a separate header button; camera detail moved from tiles into version A's status squares; a real dashboard screen was added as what you land on before choosing a center.

5

Two corrections after seeing it live. The greyed-out Cameras nav item was reversed — it should look identical to its siblings, just not navigate — and the center-search dropdown was turned into a non-functional reference: selecting a row does nothing, for now.

Two versions

A · Nested — a tab inside the camera section

Camera health as the first sub-tab, beside Live, History, Saved Clips, and Admin Display. Cheapest to ship, but the header still names one center while the page reports on fourteen.

B · Toggle — a scope above center selection

Camera health as the one thing that exists at account altitude, reached by a toggle in the header's search field. No center selected, center-scoped nav hidden. Resolves the seam honestly, at the cost of a scope the app has never had before.

B is the right long-term model; A is the right first ship. If the toggle version is likely within two quarters, build B now — retrofitting a scope later means re-teaching the navigation twice.

Layout

One row per center in both versions: identity, a fixed column of aligned status counts, then a wrapping grid of camera squares. Status is colour plus texture, never colour alone — red and green is the pair that fails for red-green colour blindness, and this board's whole meaning rides on exactly that pair.

The counts get their own column because sharing it with identity makes them wrap unpredictably; given a column, ten rows becomes one scannable ladder of digits. A 3px left edge carries each row's worst status, so the list reads before any text does.

What's different between them

  • Altitude is stated by one control. In focus mode the search field reads "All my centers" with a filled blue chip; in center scope the chip goes quiet and the field names a center.
  • The center-scoped nav hides, it doesn't grey out — absent reads as a different altitude, greyed reads as broken permissions.
  • Two entry points: the toggle for a cold start, plus a low-contrast "View across all centers" link from inside a center for someone already mid-task.
  • Cameras in the rail looks normal but isn't a route — every path to a camera runs through camera health, so there's one door, not two.
  • Detail opens in a modal, not version A's drawer — at account altitude the grid behind it belongs to one center among fourteen, so less context is worth preserving, and a modal reads as the harder stop that suits triage.
  • Healthy centers collapse into one row; problem centers show their full grid, not just the broken cameras — four red among twenty green tells a different story than four red alone.
  • Correlated failures get named: "All 4 offline cameras here run through recorder NVR-2 — likely one site network fault," hedged to "3 of the 4…" when it's partial.
  • Single-center users see none of this — the toggle and the zoom-out link are absent, not present-and-pointless, when there's nothing to aggregate.

Drill-down — clicking a center name, or "Open in center view" inside a camera's modal — sets the session's active center, lands on that center's existing Cameras page, and leaves an "← All centers" breadcrumb. No new UI; it's the page that already exists.

The refresh contract

  • The countdown is visible and the subtitle states data age — "read 12s ago".
  • Pause is real and labeled: "showing a frozen view," not a stopped clock.
  • It stops on its own when the tab is hidden, a detail view is open, or someone is typing a filter.
  • Ranking never changes under the cursor. A reorder is held behind a "Ranking changed — reorder" pill until the user leaves the list.
  • Failure is stated, not hidden — an amber banner names the data's age and offers retry; the board never goes blank.
  • Screen readers get one polite summary per read, not per-block noise.

Keyboard and access

  • Each center's grid is one tab stop with roving focus — arrows move between cameras, not 20 separate stops per center.
  • Every square's accessible name includes the duration: "Camera 14, Nap room B, offline for 1h 05m".
  • The hover card also appears on focus. Informational text sits at 4.5:1 contrast or better.

Notes for engineering

  • Don't query the assessment log directly per poll. Maintain a camera_current_status projection — one row per camera — so sorting and paginating is an indexed read, not a windowed scan.
  • Compute counts in SQL, per page. Never ship thousands of camera rows to count client-side.
  • Distinguish "offline" from "we haven't heard." A camera past its heartbeat window is unknown, not healthy — see question 2.
  • Scope every query server-side by the user's access; the UI toggle is a convenience, never the security boundary.

Open questions

1

Should the Camera health toggle carry a count?

The concept spec called a badge the main discoverability driver on a good day; the reference image had none, so it shipped without one. A middle option: show the number only when non-zero.

2

What happens to a camera whose last heartbeat is over 30 minutes old?

Right now it keeps its last status, which quietly counts a silent camera as healthy. Recommend a fourth "not reporting" state — but that widens the three-value legend.

3

Does drilling into a center from the toggle view persist as the session's center?

The prototype persists it, matching "you're now in this center's context." Recommend keeping that.

4

Timestamps in the viewer's timezone, or the center's?

Relative times ("27m ago") dodge the question for now; absolute timestamps will need an answer.

5

Is camera order within a center stable and meaningful?

If not, ordering by room would beat whatever order the log happens to return.

6

Does "degraded" carry a reason?

Low bitrate versus dropped frames versus a bad angle is the difference between calling the vendor and nudging the camera.

History, saved-clip playback, and an admin display mode are out of scope for this pass, but the sub-nav reserves Live, History, Saved Clips, and Admin Display as decorative tabs — grouped and separated by dividers — so their eventual home is visible now rather than invented later.
